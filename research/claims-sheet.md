# Sittsy — Phase 1 Claims Sheet
## Pitch prototype: "Before You Pay" (pre-payment screen for new sitters)
Researched: 17 November 2026. Every quote below is verbatim from a page actually read, max 25 words, with its source URL. Where a figure could not be verified on a primary page, that is stated explicitly — nothing is estimated.

**Method note / biggest gap:** No standalone public Money Back Promise terms page, help-centre article, or terms-of-service page on sittsy.com was found in this pass, despite targeted searches. The fullest published statement of the conditions is Sittsy's own repeated reply text on Trustpilot (company-authored, public). The prototype's premise rests on exactly this: the conditions live scattered across checkout screens and review replies, not on one canonical page a sitter can read before paying.

## 3. Red flags and sensitivities

1. **Legally live — EU consumer law is being invoked by name.** Reviewers cite EU refund law ("EU law - refund must be given if service was not provided") and one reports filing with the Italian Antitrust Authority (AGCM) over the missing pre-charge summary. The company's own defence leans on the pre-payment withdrawal waiver. Any pitch must be framed as reducing disputes, chargebacks and regulator exposure — never as "your fee is a scam" and never proposing the fee be removed (zero-commission + paid activation is their model).
   — https://www.trustpilot.com/review/sittsy.com?page=2
2. **Chargeback = permanent suspension, stated in the company's own replies.** "initiating a payment dispute or chargeback may result in permanent suspension from the platform." A reviewer reports deactivation mid-dispute, before the company's own 24-hour deadline expired. This is the most reputationally dangerous thread on the page; do not quote it at the founder as an accusation — it is context for why a clear pre-payment screen reduces escalations.
   — https://www.trustpilot.com/review/sittsy.com
3. **Alleged review-removal pressure (two independent reviewers).** One alleges a lawyer's letter with a "€50 per day" penalty for leaving a Play review up; another alleges threatening emails after reviews and legal-action warnings for "defamation". The company publicly denies conditioning anything on review removal. Treat as allegations, never as established fact, and never repeat them in the pitch.
   — https://www.trustpilot.com/review/sittsy.com and …?page=2
4. **The company replies personally, at length, and legalistically to nearly every negative Trustpilot review** — multi-paragraph rebuttals restating the conditions, correcting reviewers, and "setting the record straight". The founder-led, hands-on response style is visible in the text itself (signed variously "Sittsy Support Team" / "The Sittsy Team"). A pitch that implies the conditions are hidden will be rebutted the same way; the accurate, defensible framing is that the conditions are *scattered and stateful* (checkout screen, in-app countdowns, review replies) and can contradict each other in the app — the Padua case shows two in-app states disagreeing at once.
5. **Terminology conflict inside the company's own language:** the same payment is a "joining fee" (store listing), "activation" (replies), and "deposit" (sitters' word, echoed in company replies: "the deposit is refunded"). "Deposit" implies refundability; that word choice feeds the 24-hour-cancellation misunderstanding in Case 2.
6. **Condition-list conflict across sources:** the company's published enumeration (80% apply, reply-all-in-24h, calendar open 24-of-30, 5 testimonials, complete profile) contains **no** 7-day testimonial deadline and **no** 20-of-30-days testimonial-presence rule — yet a sitter's pre-payment screenshots reportedly showed the 20-of-30 formulation, and the company enforced a 7-day deadline in her case. Either the in-app terms differ from the reply-text terms, or they changed. For the prototype: the checklist must be a single state machine with one source of truth, because today the sources demonstrably disagree.
7. **Demand figures move and can embarrass:** the UK country total read 111 in a crawl one day before this read showed 109; NZ shows 4 requests country-wide against "12,619 sitters active". The demand data is published by Sittsy itself, which makes it safe to use — and makes the pre-payment demand picture a gift, not an attack: their own pages already tell a Brighton or Auckland sitter the truth, just *after* the marketing copy and nowhere near the Pay button.
8. **Support-reachability is a recurring secondary complaint** ("no answer", "fully automated", "couldn't reach a human"), including claims that replies land in spam — the company repeatedly instructs reviewers to check spam for support@sittsy.com. A pre-payment screen also reduces support load; that is a safe, company-aligned benefit to claim.
9. **Guarantee-figure inconsistency risk:** €20,000 is the only sourced Sittsy Guarantee figure in this pass, from company-profile copy on third-party listings, not from sittsy.com itself. US/GBP amounts were not verified. Never put a Guarantee figure in the prototype UI without re-verifying it on a primary Sittsy page for that market.
